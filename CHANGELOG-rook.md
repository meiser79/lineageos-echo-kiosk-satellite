# Echo Spot (1st gen) changelog

All notable changes to the Echo Spot (1st gen) (`rook`) image are documented here. The [Echo Dot (2nd gen)](CHANGELOG-biscuit.md), [Echo Dot (3rd gen)](CHANGELOG-donut.md), [Echo Show 5 (1st gen)](CHANGELOG-checkers.md), [Echo Show 5 (2nd gen)](CHANGELOG-cronos.md), [Echo Show 8 (1st gen)](CHANGELOG-crown.md) and [Echo Show 8 (2nd gen)](CHANGELOG-athena.md) have their own.

## 2026.11.1 - 2026-11-10

### Added
- **Echo Spot (1st gen) on LineageOS 18.1.** The repository builds R0rt1z2's amazon-oss `rook` tree with the Kiosk Satellite Edition layer the Echo Shows have: EchoService, Kiosk Satellite on first boot, Bluetooth both ways, system updates and the same debloat. The model reads Echo Spot (KS). EchoService names the Spot `echo-spot-ks` and the end of its serial on the network.
- **Kiosk Satellite gets a raw microphone**, as on the Echo Shows: microphone 0 as captured, so its own echo canceller sees the speaker's echo at a fixed delay. The image's echo canceller stays off, and `persist.vendor.amznaec.enable 1` brings it back with the Echo Show 5's settings. Amazon's ASR and Voice pipelines pass the microphones through, as on the Echo Show 5, so their echo canceller, beamformer and level normalization stay out of the way. The Spot's audio processor is older than the Shows' and has no `asp.cfg`. It reads `persist.asp.asr.passthrough` and `persist.asp.voice.passthrough` instead, which the image sets to `true`.
- **pryond runs Amazon's wake word engine from files you install**, as on the Echo Shows. The image includes none of Amazon's wake word files. With Pryon's libraries and models in `/data/pryon`, Kiosk Satellite 2026.11.14 or newer offers **Amazon** as a wake word engine. `adb shell pryond status` prints what pryond found. See [Amazon's wake word engine](docs/echo-spot.md#amazons-wake-word-engine).
- **Android's usual button on the Spot.** A short press turns the screen on and off and holding the button opens the power menu. The Spot has no privacy chip and no light that would show privacy is on, so the image has no privacy mode, and `echo_service privacy` and its `on` and `timeout` commands answer `no privacy button`. `echo_service button press` makes a hold do nothing.
- **Bluetooth both ways on the Spot.** The rook tree's Bluetooth settings left out playing to headphones and speakers and the controls for them. The Spot now takes the Echo Show 5's, so it plays to Bluetooth headphones and speakers as well as taking audio from phones.
- **Bluetooth audio keeps playing while the Bluetooth proxy scans.** Android's balanced scan, the default of Kiosk Satellite's Bluetooth proxy, scans for a second every four, and the Spot's Broadcom chip holds Bluetooth audio back during that second. Audio from phones and to headphones stuttered while the proxy ran. A fresh Spot now scans for 30 ms every 120 ms in balanced mode. The proxy's Continuous mode needed no change. The Echo Spot guide shows how to change the balanced scan.
- **A clear camera picture by day and by night.** The camera from the Echo Show 5 (1st gen) looked up its image tuning in that Show's sensor list, which has no entry for the Spot's GC0312 sensor, and the Spot's own ISP tuning from Fire OS is in an older layout than the camera reads. The picture was grey and too bright by day and red at night. The Spot now takes the Echo Show 5 (1st gen)'s ISP and exposure tuning with its own sensor values and white balance calibration. A daylight frame has the right brightness and neutral colours, and a dark one stays dark and neutral.
- **The kernel version and Android's build string name the release.** The kernel says `4.9.337-kiosksatellite-rook-<release>`, such as `4.9.337-kiosksatellite-rook-2026.11.1`, with build `#1`, as on the Echo Shows. Android's build number is the release too, as in `lineage_rook-userdebug 11 RQ3A.211001.001 2026.11.1 test-keys`, and so is the build fingerprint.
- **The nightly storage trim runs.** Android's storage maintenance, which trims `/data` and `/cache`, waits for the device to be idle and charging. The Spot is never idle that way and has no battery, so the image runs the trim once a day at 3 AM. Regular trims tell the eMMC which blocks are free, which helps it spread wear.
- **No error report every 30 minutes.** The kernel has no per-app network statistics, so Android's statistics poll would fail every 30 minutes and on every network change and write an error report to storage each time. Android reports empty statistics instead. Data usage numbers are empty on this kernel.
- **No lock sound.** With the lock screen off, Android would still play its lock sound at every boot and each time the screen turns off. The image has the lock and unlock sounds off. Settings > Sound turns them back on.
- **The Echo Spot guide.** [docs/echo-spot.md](docs/echo-spot.md) covers installing, setting up and updating the Spot, its button, the screen, volume, Bluetooth, the time server and time zone, and device commands. It notes that a single tap wakes the Spot's screen, as on Fire OS, so Android's Double tap to wake setting does nothing there. Unlocking and Getting into TWRP have sections for the Spot. [Updates from your own server](docs/offline-updates.md) covers a Spot on a network without Internet access.
